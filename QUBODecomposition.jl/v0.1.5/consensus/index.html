    Bounded serial Lagrangian consensus

    This API requires 0.1.3 or later; see installation. The historical comparison concluded revise before wider adoption, with mixed quality/work across three tiny fixtures and no general performance advantage. Its 18 attempts measured d3b778054b6da94668102d76f5716a9181350ff9, before later review fixes; those measurements are not 0.1.3 results.

    Mathematical contract

    This opt-in prototype is heuristic. Its separate certified bound targets the complete logical input objective, not constraints of a source compiled by ToQUBO. Let the stored normal form be E(x) = scale * (offset + sum_i l_i*x_i + sum_ij q_ij*x_i*x_j). Set F = s*E, where s=1 for minimization and s=-1 for maximization. The signed scale, including zero, and the offset are part of this definition. The certificate interprets each stored finite Float64 as its exact rational value; it does not first multiply or aggregate the coefficients in floating point.

    An explicit ordered plan B_1,...,B_P covers all free indices, including isolates. Each block is nonempty, has distinct valid indices, and fits max_variables. Every nonzero original interaction must have a block containing both endpoints, even at zero scale. The first containing block owns each linear term and each interaction; the global constant belongs to block 1 exactly once. No term is split. Block indices refer to the input's free-variable order after fixed-variable elimination, not its labels or its pre-elimination MOI indices.

    For every occurrence of index i introduce a copy z_pi in the original Bool {0,1} or Spin {-1,1} domain. Its reference is its first containing block. For every other occurrence impose z_pi-z_ref(i),i=0, one row of Az=0. Every original assignment has a unique equal-copy lift; conversely coverage and these equalities give exactly one original assignment. Single term ownership and the single constant imply sum_p F_p(z_p)=F(x) on this equal-copy domain. Thus the equality-constrained copied problem is equivalent to the original.

    For arbitrary finite real equality multipliers (no sign restriction), define g(lambda)=min_z [sum_p F_p(z_p)+lambda'Az]. The domain before enforcing equality is a Cartesian product, so this minimum is the sum of independent block minima. On every equal-copy lift the multiplier term vanishes. Minimizing over the larger copied domain therefore gives g(lambda) <= min_x F(x) (weak duality). Maximizing this concave function need not close the duality gap. Exact block minimizers supply the supergradient Az; heuristic block solutions or rounded surrogate minimizers do not automatically supply a supergradient for the intended dual.

    Arithmetic and evidence

    A block child receives minimization, scale one, and zero constant. Its linear and quadratic coefficients are rounded to Float64 from the exact intended surrogate. Let h be that dispatched polynomial, f the intended block polynomial without its constant, and r=f-h. For each residual term take its minimum over the original domain independently; their sum R satisfies r(z)>=R. A completely scanned public OPTIMAL child, under its MOI promise to solve the dispatched objective globally, supplies a minimizing returned state z* and hence min f >= h(z*)+R. Add the exact owned block constant, then sum all blocks. All these operations use exact rationals. If coefficients cannot be dispatched finitely, the call fails conservatively. Missing, interrupted, local, approximate or unsupported certificate evidence never supplies a block lower bound. A heuristic incumbent energy is an upper bound on a block minimum, not a lower bound. ExactSampler on QUBODrivers 0.6.5 reports uncertified LOCALLY_SOLVED. Under the corrected 0.6.6 public contract it can supply supported block certificates, subject to the same validation and rounding corrections. This does not promote consensus completion to an original global certificate. No additional solver capability or reported ObjectiveBound is inferred.

    When every residual variable coefficient is zero, exact child minima also minimize the intended surrogate. Otherwise the residual bound remains valid but copy disagreement is only a heuristic update direction. Constants are restored exactly even though children never receive them. The certificate assumes the child honors the mathematical MOI objective it is given; public status cannot validate an arbitrary dishonest child.

    Original candidates are reconciled by taking each variable's reference copy. This yields a complete domain assignment even when copies disagree. The parent independently evaluates its original energy and compares exact rational F values to retain the best incumbent, including under cancellation. Scalar output energies follow the existing original Float64 evaluation contract. Certified bounds and gaps are kept separately as exact rational values in normalized minimization units. No tolerance creates validity or closes a gap.

    Every completed iteration is a transaction: all block scans, reconciliation, original evaluation and certificate work finish before publishing its candidate, bound and history. Partial iterations preserve earlier valid evidence. Completion, copy agreement, exact children and even a zero bound gap keep heuristic status; the independently justified constant-objective route remains OPTIMAL.

    Configuration, budgets and results

    Use strategy=:consensus, consensus_plan=[[1,2],[2,3]], and an ordinary zero-argument child_optimizer factory. The ordered plan is copied on set/get; indices inside each block are sorted, while block order fixes references and ownership. There is no whole-model shortcut or child-generated starting solution. The complete input start (default zeros for Bool, minus ones for Spin) is evaluated before the strategy runs. Invalid plans are rejected before child dispatch, including when the objective is constant or its scale is zero.

    Multipliers start at Float64 zero. After completed iteration k, update every row by lambda <- clamp(lambda + consensus_step/sqrt(k) * disagreement, -consensus_multiplier_limit, consensus_multiplier_limit) in Float64. Defaults are consensus_step=1.0, consensus_multiplier_limit=1e6, and consensus_iterations=16. Every finite stored multiplier is interpreted exactly in the next surrogate. Nonfinite proposals stop with NORM_LIMIT and preserve previous evidence. Projection, bounded work and approximate directions do not promise convergence. Copy agreement stops immediately. Otherwise stop after consensus_stagnation=4 consecutive completed iterations improving neither the exact original incumbent nor the best certified bound, or the iteration cap. These normal completion routes return heuristic LOCALLY_SOLVED.

    This update is projected dual supergradient ascent; see Multiplier update references. With L(z,lambda)=sum_p F_p(z_p)+lambda'Az and an exact intended-Lagrangian minimizer z_k, g(mu) <= g(lambda_k) + (Az_k)'(mu-lambda_k) for every mu. Thus Az_k is a supergradient of the concave dual, giving the plus sign in lambda_next = projection(lambda_k + alpha_k*Az_k). Here each row of Az_k is the non-reference copy minus its reference copy. The schedule alpha_k=consensus_step/sqrt(k) is a standard nonsummable diminishing step size. The symmetric box projection and its default limit are prototype safeguards; equality multipliers themselves have no sign restriction. The cited exact supergradient argument applies to discrete block domains as well, but does not justify heuristic or rounding-altered directions, primal optimality, or convergence under this prototype's finite budgets and stopping rules.

    All iterations share max_child_calls, max_candidate_evaluations, the relative parent time limit and optional absolute deadline. Each scanned block row consumes one parent candidate evaluation, as does each reconciled original candidate; the initial original evaluation consumes one. This strategy's counter therefore includes surrogate evaluations. Exhaustion or interruptions retain only completed iteration transactions. Child calls remain cooperative with the existing time limit forwarding; an arbitrary child can overrun. Preparation, exact certificate work and coordination are charged to parent wall time, and measured in the preparation, bound, coordination, copying, execution, validation_reconstruction and full_energy phase totals.

    metadata(solution)["decomposition"]["consensus"] exposes the canonical blocks, term owners, equality rows, iteration counts, best certified best_lower_bound, incumbent_normalized, exact absolute_gap, certificate method and bounded history. These rational values are in normalized minimization units; for a maximization input negate the lower bound to obtain an original-objective upper bound. A missing certificate is nothing, never a heuristic surrogate value. History records evaluated multipliers, copy solutions, disagreement, original candidate/energy and whether a genuine supergradient was certified. Retain at most consensus_history_limit=16 iterations (oldest discarded); best evidence survives eviction. Calls identify their dispatched block and public child status. The three integer consensus options accept 1:10000, and the step and multiplier limit must be finite positive Float64-representable values.

    Only serial execution, default selection, and no partition/separator/pruning options are supported with this strategy; incompatible combinations fail before dispatch. Conditional repair, automatic overlapping plans, parallel coordination, preprocessing integration and source network constraints are deferred. Defaults and existing strategies retain their behavior.

    The executable offline example uses a guarded rational exhaustive child with public OPTIMAL, without altering released ExactSampler's status. Run julia --project=. examples/consensus/offline.jl. The bounded comparison records candidate quality, certificates, complete timing and total work separately.

    Multiplier update references

    1. Stephen Boyd, Lin Xiao, Almir Mutapcic, and Jacob Mattingley, Notes on Decomposition Methods, May 11, 2015, section 2, pp. 7–8. Introduces local copies and equality consistency constraints, then updates their multipliers using copy disagreement. Its update nu := nu - alpha_k*(y2-y1) matches our lambda += alpha_k*(copy-reference) when y1=copy and y2=reference.
    2. Stephen Boyd, with help from Jaehyun Park, Subgradient Methods, May 2014, section 2.2, pp. 4–5, and sections 6–6.2, pp. 21–24. Gives alpha_k=a/sqrt(k) as a nonsummable diminishing schedule and develops projected subgradient methods, including ascent on the Lagrangian dual. These are references for the update's components; they do not establish convergence of this bounded heuristic implementation.
